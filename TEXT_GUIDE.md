# Coworker Guide: Reviewing Your Tips & Filing a California Wage Claim
### (No AI needed — do it yourself, step by step)

> This is the do-it-yourself alternative to the Muse prompt. Same goal,
> same official process — just you and this guide instead of an AI helper.

## What this is

A plain-language walkthrough for current and former HOPE Dog Training
(Norco, CA) employees who want to check whether customer tips were paid to
them correctly, and — only if they choose — prepare and file a California
wage claim. Voluntary. You decide whether to file. This is information, not
legal advice, and not a government service.

## Why isn't Zach sharing what he saw?

Zach has seen records that concern him, but he's not passing them around —
and that's deliberate. Those records contain customer names, payment
details, and other people's private information. Distributing them could
expose him legally and could put *you* at risk too if you relied on
records that aren't yours. What's yours is yours: **your** paystubs,
**your** notes, **your** messages, **your** memory of what you saw
firsthand. That's what a claim is built on — and it's stronger for it.

## Step 1 — Write down your employment basics

- Are you still employed at HOPE? Dates you worked there (approximate is fine).
- Your job title(s), department(s), and approximate shifts.
- The employer's legal name and address **exactly as shown on your
  paystub**. Copy it — don't guess. If you don't know it, that's OK; note
  that it's unknown.

## Step 2 — Get your paystubs

Your paystubs show what you were paid and whether tips were included.

**QuickBooks Workforce phone app:** sign in → tap **Money** → open **All
Paychecks** → adjust the date range → open a paycheck → tap **Download**
if available.
**Website:** sign in at workforce.intuit.com → open **Paychecks** → choose
your date range.
Official guide: https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/t4-slips/view-paychecks-w-2s-quickbooks-workforce/L3TCwUJtD_US_en_US

This only works if HOPE set up Workforce and invited you. No luck? Use
paystubs you already have, request copies of your payroll records, or note
which records are missing — the Labor Commissioner can ask the employer
for them. Never share your QuickBooks password with anyone.

## Step 3 — Write down what you remember about tips

Answer honestly, in your own words. "I don't know" is always a valid
answer — never guess:

1. Did customers leave tips for your work, or for staff generally? How do
   you know — what did you see or hear *directly*?
2. Did you ever receive tips through payroll (on your paycheck)?
3. Did you receive tips separately — cash, Zelle, another app? About how
   much, in total? (Only if you truly remember.)
4. Did customers ever tip you directly?
5. Did management ever explain a tip-sharing arrangement? What did they say,
   in your own words — or was nothing ever explained?
6. Were tips assigned to specific workers, or pooled and shared?
7. Do you remember specific times you believe a tip wasn't paid to you?
   Write what happened, with dates if you can.
8. Do you have messages, notes, or screenshots of your own about tips?

## Step 4 — Gather your evidence (what matters most)

The strongest evidence, roughly in order:

1. **Your paystubs** — especially whether a tip line appears or is missing.
   (A missing tip line supports your case; it isn't proof on its own.)
2. **Your W-2s and personal payment histories** (bank/app statements).
3. **Your own notes and messages** — texts, emails, or notes about tips,
   written when things happened if possible.
4. **Your employment documents** — offer letter, handbook, any written tip
   policy.
5. **Your firsthand account** — what you personally saw and heard, written
   down clearly with dates.

Do NOT try to get customer invoices, reservation histories, POS records,
or anyone else's pay information. Your claim stands on your records.

For each item, note whether it's **documented** (you have the record),
a **firsthand account** (you saw/heard it), **corroborated** (something
else confirms it), an **estimate** (your best recollection), or **unknown**.

## Step 5 — Write your statement

In your own words, first person, one page:

> My name is [___]. I worked at [employer legal name and address, from
> paystub] from [dates] as a [title] in [department]. My schedule was
> approximately [shifts].
>
> Tips I received through payroll: [amount / none that I recall / unknown].
> Tips I received separately: [amount and method / none / unknown].
> What management told me about tip sharing: [their words / "nothing was
> explained to me"].
>
> Specific incidents I remember: [describe, or "none specific"].
> Amount I believe I was not paid: [honest figure, or "unknown"].
>
> Evidence I have: [list]. Records I'm missing that my employer may have:
> [paystubs for certain periods, tip distribution records, tip policies…].
>
> This statement is based on my own records and recollection. Where I am
> uncertain, I have said so rather than estimating.

## Step 6 — File your claim

Official starting point: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/HowToFileWageClaim.htm

- **How:** online ("File Wage or Garment Claim Online"), or by email,
  mail, or in person at a local Labor Commissioner office.
- **Attach:** copies of your paystubs and supporting documents — **never
  send originals**.
- **Deadlines matter:** time limits are generally 1–4 years depending on
  the claim type. Verify yours — don't assume.
- **What happens next:** a deputy reviews your claim; most cases go to a
  settlement conference first; if unresolved, a hearing is scheduled, then
  an Order, Decision, or Award. Either side can appeal.
- **Save** your confirmation or case number. For updates, email the
  district office where you filed with your claim number in the subject
  line.
- If the amount or legal basis is uncertain, contact the DLSE or a
  qualified employment attorney **before** submitting.

What to attach (official list): https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/dlseSupportingDocs.html

## Step 7 — After filing

- Track your filing date, method, confirmation/case number, any documents
  requested, and conference/hearing dates. Bring every notice you receive —
  conferences and hearings may require you to attend.
- **Retaliation is illegal.** If you're fired, demoted, lose hours, or are
  threatened after asking about tips or filing, you can file a retaliation
  complaint (usually within 1 year): https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/RetaliationComplaintProcedure.htm
  Write down dates, what happened, and witnesses privately, right away.
- You do **not** need to quit your job to file a claim.

## What if we all file?

Honest answer: nobody can promise you an amount or an outcome — not this
guide, not Zach, not anyone. But here's what changes when several people
file independent claims about the same pattern:

- One claim can be brushed off as a misunderstanding. Five or ten people
  describing the same thing, with their own paystubs and their own
  timelines, is a **pattern** — and patterns are what trigger real
  investigations.
- Each claim is still decided on its **own** evidence. Filing doesn't help
  you if your own records don't support it — so be accurate, not
  optimistic.
- A business-wide violation report (different from your claim:
  https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/HowToReportViolationtoBOFE.htm) asks the
  state to investigate the business itself. It doesn't automatically get
  *you* paid — that's what your individual wage claim is for. You can do
  either, both, or neither.

Bottom line: the more people who accurately document what happened to
*them*, the harder the full picture is to ignore. But file your own
truthful claim — don't copy someone else's.

## Your rights, in brief (California)

- Tips left by customers generally belong to the employee(s) they were
  left for. Employers and their agents may not keep any portion (Labor
  Code §351).
- Credit-card tips must generally reach you by the next regular payday,
  with no deductions for processing fees.
- Tip pooling can be lawful if fair and reasonable — but owners, managers,
  and supervisors generally may not take a share.
- Your job title alone doesn't decide everything.
- Official FAQ: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_TipsAndGratuities.html
