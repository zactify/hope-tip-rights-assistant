# Legal resources (official California sources)

All links verified live on 2026-10-13. Re-verify quarterly — DIR
occasionally moves pages. These are the only legal authorities the app
cites; the app does not invent legal rules.

## Tips and gratuities

- **Tips and gratuities FAQ** — California Department of Industrial
  Relations, Division of Labor Standards Enforcement (DLSE):
  https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/FAQ_TipsAndGratuities.html
  - Covers: tips are the employee's property; employers/agents may not keep
    or share in them (Labor Code § 351); credit-card tips paid by next
    regular payday; no deductions for processing fees; lawful tip pooling
    rules (fair, reasonable; owners/managers/supervisors may not share).

## Filing a wage claim

- **How to file a wage claim:**
  https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/HowToFileWageClaim.htm
  - Online filing, filing by email/mail/in person, what to gather first,
    filing deadlines (1/2/3/4 years depending on claim type), office
    locator.
- **Supporting documents for a wage claim:**
  https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/dlseSupportingDocs.html
  - Time records, paystubs, bounced checks, employment notices; submit
    copies, never originals.

## Business-wide violations

- **Report a labor law violation (Bureau of Field Enforcement):**
  https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/HowToReportViolationtoBOFE.htm
  - Separate from an individual wage claim; reports kept confidential to
    the maximum extent possible under the law.

## Retaliation

- **Retaliation complaint procedure:**
  https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/RetaliationComplaintProcedure.htm
  - Who may file (employees, former employees, applicants); generally a
    one-year deadline from the adverse action; online or paper (RCI 1)
    filing; investigation overview.

## Key legal points the app communicates (plain language)

1. In California, tips left by customers generally belong to the employee(s)
   they were left for — employers and their agents may not keep them.
2. Tip pooling can be lawful if fair and reasonable, but owners, managers,
   and supervisors generally may not take a share.
3. A job title alone does not conclusively decide who may receive tips.
4. Employees do not need to resign before filing a wage claim.
5. Retaliation for protected wage activity is prohibited.
6. Filing deadlines vary by claim type and legal theory — verify, don't
   assume.
7. No recovery amount or outcome is promised.
8. Uncertain amounts or legal basis → contact the DLSE or a qualified
   employment attorney before submitting.

> This project provides legal *information*, not legal *advice*.
